There are two honest paths to wearing gele: learn to tie it yourself from a long strip of fabric, or wear an auto-gele — pre-pleated, mounted on a firm base, and secured with a Velcro or elastic strap like a hat. Neither is the "wrong" choice. Here's how the real tying method works, and when auto-gele is genuinely the better call.

## Traditional Gele vs. Auto-Gele

**Traditional gele** is a flat, long strip of fabric you wrap, pleat, and fold by hand — no pre-set shape, which is exactly why it takes practice. The classic fabric is **aso oke**, stiff enough to hold a sharp pleat, though it's genuinely harder to handle for a first attempt. **Ankara, damask, and jacquard** are common, more beginner-friendly alternatives — softer and more forgiving while you're still learning the folds.

**Auto-gele** is a real, distinct product, not a shortcut version of the same thing — it comes pre-pleated on a firm base and goes on more like a hat than something you tie, secured at the back with Velcro or an elastic strap. It needs some adjusting for fit, but no tying skill at all. If you've never tied gele before, there's no shame in starting here.

## What Fabric Length You Need

This depends on what you're using. Pre-cut, ready-to-tie gele fabric typically runs **around 2.2-2.5 meters**. If you're using raw aso oke strip cloth instead, expect to need roughly **6 meters**, since it's narrower loom-width fabric sewn into a usable length.

## The Classic Method, Step by Step

This is the standard approach for a classic round/fan-style gele — treat it as the reliable starting method, not the only way it's ever done, since real technique varies with the specific style and a bit of personal habit:

1. **Prep your hair first.** Pack it back smoothly or use a wig cap — you need a stable, even base for the gele to sit on.
2. **Fold in one end of the fabric strip** to create a smooth, clean edge at the crown of your head.
3. **Wrap the fabric around your head** from back to front, keeping both ends roughly equal in length at your forehead.
4. **Take one side and wrap it toward the opposite ear**, holding it firmly — this becomes your shorter end.
5. **Cross the longer side over it** and wrap that around to the back of your head.
6. **Begin shaping the layers** at the side and back, squeezing the fabric inward to start forming the fan silhouette, then repeat the same shaping on the other side.
7. **Tie the two ends closest to your head into a double knot** at the back, and tuck any loose fabric in neatly.
8. **Arrange the back layer** so the highest point of the fabric sits centered.
9. **Pleat the front fabric into even folds**, building up toward the signature standing peak — this is the step that takes the most practice, and honestly, exact finger position and pleat count is something you'll develop a feel for rather than follow as a fixed formula.
10. **Gently push the gele back and slightly away from your face** for lift, without overdoing it, keep your ears tucked in evenly, and check the shape in a mirror before you're done.

## Common Mistakes

- **An uneven first fold** at step 2 throws off everything that follows — take the extra few seconds to get that base right.
- **Tying too tight** — it should feel secure, not like it's giving you a headache by the time you leave the house.
- **Loose stray hairs** at the front, from not packing hair back tightly enough in step 1.
- **Pushing the gele too far back** — a little lift looks intentional; too much risks it slipping off entirely.

## Named Styles You'll Come Across

As you get more comfortable, you'll start hearing specific style names — fan gele, bow gele, round gele, pleated gele, infinity gele, rose gele, turban gele — each with its own signature shape and finish. ## If You'd Rather Skip Tying Altogether

There's nothing wrong with reaching for an auto-gele instead, especially for an early morning event or if tying just isn't a skill you want to build right now. It's a legitimate, commonly worn option in its own right, not a lesser substitute — plenty of people who know how to tie traditional gele still keep an auto-gele on hand for convenience.
