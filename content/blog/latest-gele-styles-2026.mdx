Big, bold gele is back. Multiple independent sources point the same direction right now: dramatic height, sculpted structure, and sharp pleating are what's trending, a real shift away from smaller, more modest shapes — and it's showing up specifically at owambe parties and weddings, not just bridal party lineups.

Here's what's actually current, beyond the classic styles we cover in [our tying tutorial](/blog/how-to-tie-gele).

## The Shapes Trending Right Now

Beyond the classic fan, round, and pleated styles, current coverage names several more dramatic shapes in circulation: **peacock gele**, **ruffled gele**, **beaded gele**, **flower gele**, **infinity pleat** and **infinity step gele**, and **royal or crown gele**. The common thread across all of them is structure and volume — side fans, layered petals, and sharp, sweeping pleats rather than a simple, low-profile wrap. If you're getting one tied for an event right now, this is the direction to reference.

![A woman wearing a tall, glossy pink satin gele tied with a bow-like fan structure at the front, with defined sharp pleats](/images/blog/gele-2026-pink.jpg)

*A different color and fold structure from the cover, but the same current direction — real height, sharp defined pleats.*

Color doesn't seem to be following its own independent trend the way the shapes are — geles are typically color-matched to the rest of the outfit (aso oke, buba and iro, or aso-ebi fabric) rather than chasing a color trend of their own. If your outfit fabric is set, your gele color follows from that, not the other way around.

## Fabric

Aso oke remains the top choice for a structured, dramatic gele, alongside **sego**, ankara, brocade, and adire — the stiffer the fabric, the better it holds the sharp pleats current styling calls for. If you're leaning toward one of the bigger, more sculpted shapes above, ask your tailor or gele stylist about fabric stiffness specifically, since a softer fabric won't hold that shape as well.

## Auto-Gele Right Now

Auto-gele's popularity as a category keeps growing — it's a real, increasingly common choice, especially when convenience matters more than the specific shape. Beyond that, though, we couldn't find reliably sourced detail on specific auto-gele style trends for this year, so we're not going to hand you invented style names — if you're shopping for one, ask to see current stock in person rather than searching for a named "2026 auto-gele style."

## What It Costs

Real pricing is hard to pin down beyond one Nigerian wedding-vendor source, so treat this as a starting reference rather than a fixed number: a quick, basic gele tie runs roughly **₦3,000-₦10,000**, an event gele **₦5,000-₦20,000**, bridal gele tying **₦20,000-₦120,000**, and premium or designer work **₦100,000 and up**. Get an actual quote from whoever's tying it for you — pricing varies a lot by stylist and occasion.

## Ordering Yours

If you're going for one of the bigger, more sculpted shapes trending right now, mention that specifically when you're sourcing fabric — a stiffer aso oke or sego will hold the shape through a full event, where a softer fabric might not survive the night.
