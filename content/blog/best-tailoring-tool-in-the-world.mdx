## 2. Market-Grade Offline Resilience (Works Anywhere in the World)

One of the largest vulnerabilities of modern cloud software is fragile network dependency. A tailor cutting fabric in an underground workshop in Milan or an open-air market stall in Tejuosho, Lagos, cannot afford to have their workshop grind to a halt because of a dropped cell tower or internet outage.

MyStitchBook features an **Offline-First Outbox Architecture**:
1. When you measure a client or update an order's stage while offline, the data is saved instantly to local browser and device storage (IndexedDB).
2. The UI updates with zero latency — no freezing, no spinning wheels, no blank screens.
3. High-resolution cloth swatches and style inspiration photos compress locally on the device.
4. The moment the device reconnects to Wi-Fi or cellular signal, all queued measurements, stage progressions, and financial records synchronize deterministically to the cloud with zero data loss.

## 3. The Cutting Table: Bridging Front-of-House with Back-of-House

Most tailoring systems stop at invoicing. Once the customer pays, the software goes dark, leaving the cutting table to rely on chalk on wood, lost paper slips, and frantic workshop phone calls.

MyStitchBook treats the **Cutting Table** as the heart of the workshop:
- **Cloth Shears Queue:** Raw fabric bolts and client fabrics are logged with visual swatches, yardage allowances, and priority tags (`Normal`, `Urgent`, `Rush`).
- **One-Tap Workshop Cutting Slips:** Cutters receive precise, formatted cutting specs with exact measurements and client notes, completely segregated from sensitive customer billing data.
- **Stage Progression Kanban:** Workshop managers track each order through five clear artisan phases:
  1. **Documented:** Intake complete, fabric inspected, measurements verified.
  2. **Cutting:** Cloth shears allocated, pattern drafted, canvas basted.
  3. **Sewing:** Assembly by machinists and tailors.
  4. **Fitting / Ready:** Garment prepared for client try-on or final press.
  5. **Delivered:** Handover confirmed, final settlement reconciled.

## 5. Piece-Rate Workshop Payroll & Craft Role Delegation

Bespoke craft rooms do not operate on simple hourly clock-ins. A thriving workshop employs a collaborative team of artisans paid through complex combinations of monthly salaries, percentages, and piece-rate pay:
- **Master Cutters** paid per garment drafted.
- **Machinists / Tailors** paid per completed seam.
- **Finishers & Pressers** compensated per finished piece.
- **Beading & Embroidery Artisans** paid per ornate embellishment panel.

MyStitchBook includes an automated **Piece-Rate Payroll Engine**. As garments transition through the Kanban columns, artisan payouts calculate automatically, generating itemized payroll summaries with complete audit histories.
