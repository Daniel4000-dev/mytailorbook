MyStitchBook treats the **Cutting Table** as the heart of the workshop:

## Tailoring & Fashion Business 101: The Master Workshop Playbook

Whether you are in your early tailoring beginnings or scaling an established bespoke fashion brand, running a profitable workshop requires mastering both the art of the shears and the systems of modern business. Below are the core operational principles every tailor and fashion designer must know:

### 1. Tailoring Beginning: Moving from Paper Notebooks to Digital Systems
In the early days of tailoring, most artisans record customer measurements in paper exercise books. Within six months, pages tear, chalk stains obscure numbers, and when a returning customer calls for an urgent outfit while you are at the fabric market, you are stranded without their specs.
- **The Golden Rule:** Adopt an easy digital tailor app on day one.
- **Why it matters:** Recording measurements digitally with anatomical tags (neck, chest, half-length, inseam, outseam) guarantees that customer records remain permanently accessible, backed up, and search-ready from your phone.

### 2. How to Grow and Scale a Tailoring Business
Scaling from a solo artisan to a thriving fashion house requires dividing labor and eliminating bottlenecks:
- **Specialized Division of Labor:** Do not let your master cutters assemble zippers or sew buttons. Separate your workflow into distinct craft stages: Pattern Drafting & Cutting, Machine Assembly, Finishing & Handwork, and Quality Control.
- **Automate Piece-Rate Payroll:** Compensating artisans per completed seam or garment drafted keeps throughput high, prevents wage disputes, and aligns team incentives directly with prompt client delivery.
- **Multi-Branch Operations:** As demand grows from one studio into secondary intake salons or dedicated production facilities, centralize your client directory, fabric inventory, and order statuses in a unified cloud platform.

### 3. How to Improve Customer Service as a Fashion Designer or Tailor
The number one reason tailoring clients switch designers is **communication breakdown and late delivery anxiety**. When clients have to call five times to ask, *"Is my cloth ready?"*, trust erodes.
- **Transparent Digital Receipts:** Never scribble prices on scrap paper. Issue an instant, branded digital receipt over WhatsApp with clear payment breakdowns, deposit records, and agreed completion dates.
- **Live Self-Service Tracking:** Give clients a private web link where they can see their garment move from Cutting to Sewing and Fitting without interrupting your workshop's workday.
- **Structured Fitting Sessions:** Treat try-ons as technical consultations. Record chalk pin alterations and fit notes directly into the customer's permanent file so subsequent orders fit seamlessly on the first attempt.

### 4. How to Get Customers as a Tailor
Word of mouth remains powerful, but modern high-paying clients evaluate tailors through digital proof of craft:
- **Curate a Digital Lookbook:** Instead of sending uncompressed photo dumps over WhatsApp chat, share a clean digital catalogue organized by style silhouette (Senator Suits, Agbada, Kaftans, Two-Piece Sets, Safari Suits).
- **Showcase the Craft Process:** High-end clients appreciate the discipline behind bespoke construction. Document the shears at the cutting table, the canvas basting, and the precision collar plackets.
- **Reactivate Lapsed Clients:** Keep track of clients whose last order was over 90 days ago. A polite seasonal touchpoint with new textile swatches frequently generates repeat commissions.

### 5. Must-Know Fundamentals for Managing a Fashion Business
1. **Never cut without verified measurements:** Always cross-check customer posture, shoe heel height, and fit ease preference (slim, regular, or relaxed) before shears touch cloth.
2. **Reconcile payments daily:** Balance cash receipts, POS terminal settlements, and bank transfers every evening to eliminate unrecorded workshop leakage.
3. **Track fabric bolt inventory:** Always log fabric yardage upon arrival to prevent yardage shortages halfway through cutting.
